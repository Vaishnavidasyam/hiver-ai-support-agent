FAIL 01 • CRITICAL
Multi-Intent Overload
CUSTOMER QUERY
"Your Amazon.in Order # 404-3072127 double charged and no one helps. Returns were not collected and now this."
OBSERVED BEHAVIOR

Classified as delivery_delay_tracking & auto-handled.

EXPECTED BEHAVIOR

payment_billing_issue + mandatory human escalation.

ROOT CAUSE

Order ID regex and delivery tokens diluted the financial dispute tokens in single-label centroids, masking the urgent charge dispute.

MITIGATION:

Multi-label intent detection or high-priority financial trigger regex override to force escalation.

FAIL 02 • HIGH
Sarcasm & Hashtag Bias
CUSTOMER QUERY
"so well for amazon prime! Still waiting for my order. Disappointed #AmazonPrime"
OBSERVED BEHAVIOR

Classified as prime_membership_benefits & auto-handled.

EXPECTED BEHAVIOR

Delivery delay complaint with escalation intake.

ROOT CAUSE

Sarcasm inverted sentiment; explicit hashtag #AmazonPrime heavily biased vector similarity toward subscription benefits.

MITIGATION:

Strip promotional hashtags during normalization; apply contextual negation weighting.

FAIL 03 • MEDIUM
Entangled Cross-Domain Intents
CUSTOMER QUERY
"@AmazonHelp since when is Prime a week for delivery? Hmmm...."
OBSERVED BEHAVIOR

Predicted Prime benefits instead of delivery delay tracking.

EXPECTED BEHAVIOR

Shipping transit delay dispute.

ROOT CAUSE

Query straddles Prime SLA terms and delivery execution; single-label classification forced an artificial winner.

MITIGATION:

Dual-label composite tagging to generate hybrid SLA response.

FAIL 04 • SAFE FALLBACK
Terse Financial Query
CUSTOMER QUERY
"What is going on with my recent transaction??"
OBSERVED BEHAVIOR

Classified as unknown_ambiguous (Confidence: 0.547 < 0.58), safely escalated.

EXPECTED BEHAVIOR

Identify specific billing intent while escalating.

ROOT CAUSE

Extreme brevity lowered similarity below 0.58 confidence threshold; safety fallback worked, but classification was coarse.

MITIGATION:

Elevate prototype similarity when financial terms ('transaction') are detected.

FAIL 05 • KNOWLEDGE DRIFT
Historical Policy & Workflow Drift
CUSTOMER QUERY
"How do I return this third party seller item without a printer?"
HISTORICAL PRECEDENT (2017 TWITTER CORPUS)

Advised the customer to DM their address so Amazon could email a printable return label PDF attachment.

CURRENT REALITY (OBSOLETE ADVICE)

Modern Amazon policy uses QR-code label-less drop-offs at UPS Stores / Whole Foods, making 2017 historical advice completely obsolete.

CRITICAL SYSTEM LIMITATION • Historical precedent ≠ current policy

Grounding purely in historical resolution data carries temporal latency without real-time policy overrides and freshness filtering.

MITIGATION:

Metadata-based policy override filters and recency decay scoring in the FAISS retrieval layer to suppress outdated historical procedures.

ENGINEERING DECISION LOG

Architectural Decision Records (15 Records)

Deliberate architectural choices, alternatives considered, trade-offs, and experimental rationale.

DECISION

Selected @AmazonHelp (81,092 conversations) instead of airline or telecom handles.

WHY / RATIONALE

Retail e-commerce exhibits grounded operational intents (returns, tracking) alongside acute safety triggers (compromised accounts, OTP theft, card disputes). Airline data is heavily distorted by seasonal weather cancellations.

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

High customer volume required careful deduplication and English language filtering.

DECISION

Split train/test strictly by conversation_id, completely isolating historical threads.

WHY / RATIONALE

Shuffling individual tweets leaks historical replies from the same thread into the FAISS index, invalidating test results.

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

0 conversation ID overlap between training and golden test sets (verified by automated test).

DECISION

Derived a custom 9-intent + UNKNOWN retail taxonomy rather than forcing Banking77.

WHY / RATIONALE

Banking77 is fintech-focused (ATM fees, card PINs). Retail Twitter support is driven by physical logistics, carrier delays, and returns.

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

Accurate domain taxonomy representing true retail operations.

DECISION

Capped taxonomy at 9 domain classes + 1 explicit unknown category.

WHY / RATIONALE

Support workflows require actionability, not hyper-fine categorization. Agents need to know whether to initiate a carrier trace or escalate to fraud.

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

Prevented sparse class collapse while maintaining distinct routing outcomes.

DECISION

Rejection threshold of 0.58 routes ambiguous queries to unknown_ambiguous.

WHY / RATIONALE

Real social support messages are notoriously terse ("Still waiting.", "Hello?"). A model forced into a closed set will hallucinate an arbitrary intent with false certainty.

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

Controlled uncertainty triggers safe human escalation.

DECISION

Used in-memory flat inner product FAISS on normalized embeddings.

WHY / RATIONALE

Reviewers must reproduce results locally in under 15 minutes without external API credentials, cost, or network latency.

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

<2ms vector query latency and 100% offline reproducibility.

DECISION

Subsampled 8,000 clean, unique, English conversation pairs for FAISS.

WHY / RATIONALE

Assignment explicitly encouraged subsampling: "We will not run your code on the full dataset — a subsample is expected and encouraged."

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

Dense semantic coverage while keeping RAM under 300MB and build time under 3 minutes.

DECISION

Built an explicit EvidenceLayer between retrieval and generation.

WHY / RATIONALE

Standard RAG pipelines blindly pass retrieved documents to generation. If precedents have weak similarity or conflicting resolutions, unconstrained models hallucinate policies.

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

Requires top similarity ≥ 0.55 and precedent consensus ≥ 0.60 before permitting automated replies.

DECISION

Strict programmatic human escalation for account_security_login and payment_billing_issue.

WHY / RATIONALE

Never allow an autonomous model to handle password resets or disputed charges. Autonomous policy promises on stolen cards pose legal liability.

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

100% deterministic escalation on sensitive domains.

DECISION

Optimized the decision policy to minimize False Auto-Handles (3.8%) rather than maximize raw automation rate.

WHY / RATIONALE

Unnecessary human escalation costs $3; unsafe automation of a security breach or fraudulent charge costs thousands in fines and churn.

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

High safety posture capturing 96.2% (51/53) of critical escalation queries.

DECISION

Evaluated intent classification using Macro F1 as the primary metric.

WHY / RATIONALE

Class imbalance in retail Twitter is extreme (delivery tracking >35%). A majority classifier achieves ~35% accuracy but near-zero Macro F1 (0.0261).

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

Macro F1 penalizes models that fail on rare or sensitive categories.

DECISION

Evaluated the automated judge against 60 independent human annotations.

WHY / RATIONALE

Machine evaluators suffer from self-preference and leniency bias. Validating correlation proves the rubric reflects human engineering standards.

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

Spearman correlation of 0.7632 (p < 0.0001), 81.7% close agreement, and Cohen's kappa of 0.6575.

DECISION

Synthesized replies by adapting verified historical precedents rather than calling external commercial LLM APIs.

WHY / RATIONALE

Commercial LLM APIs introduce non-deterministic outputs, latency (2-5s), and quota exhaustions that break reviewer test runs.

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

Grounded synthesis guarantees deterministic, 100% reproducible replies in milliseconds.

DECISION

Built a curated set of 200 examples (within required 150-250 range) stratified across 10 intents.

WHY / RATIONALE

200 examples balances statistical power (standard error < 3.5%) with meticulous manual review of every label and guideline.

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

Balanced coverage across 10 intents, multi-turn contexts, and sensitive escalation cases.

DECISION

Automated regex sanitization stripping historical customer names ("Declan", "Dhrumil") and raw placeholders ("[URL]").

WHY / RATIONALE

Leaking an unrelated customer's name from 2017 into a new response violates privacy and looks unprofessional.

TRADE-OFF & RESULT

Clean, professional greetings with standard portal links (amazon.com/orders) and canonical ^AH sign-off.

RESEARCH INSIGHT • Engineering Analysis

WHAT IS MISLEADING ABOUT MY HEADLINE NUMBER?

The Headline Paradox

Looking only at Macro F1, one might assume Baseline 2 is superior. That conclusion is incomplete for customer-support automation.

Baseline 2 0.7813 Macro F1
Proposed Agent 0.5913 Macro F1
"The proposed system introduces explicit uncertainty rejection and risk-aware escalation. Therefore closed-set Macro F1 alone does not fully capture automation safety."
1. Classification Accuracy ≠ Customer Safety

Baseline 2 memorized n-grams, achieving higher F1 but mistakenly auto-handling 22.6% of sensitive escalation queries (unauthorized charges and account breaches). The proposed agent dropped false auto-handles to 3.8%.

2. Safe Rejection Can Lower Closed-Set F1

The model uses a calibrated rejection threshold (0.58). Terse or ambiguous queries safely route to unknown_ambiguous to trigger human review. Closed-set multi-class scoring penalizes this safety fallback as an intent classification error.
